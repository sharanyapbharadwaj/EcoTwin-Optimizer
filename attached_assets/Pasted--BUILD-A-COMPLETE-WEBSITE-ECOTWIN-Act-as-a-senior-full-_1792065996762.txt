## 4. MODULE ONE: COLD-CHAIN WAREHOUSE DIGITAL TWIN

Scenario: A refrigerated food warehouse maintains a target temperature of -18°C. Refrigeration is energy-intensive, and thermal loads vary with weather and operating conditions.

Interface:
- Warehouse zone heatmap with multiple storage zones.
- Temperature readings and target temperature.
- Refrigeration power in kW and energy in kWh.
- Outside temperature and electricity tariff.
- Temperature trend chart.
- Normal cooling versus pre-cooling comparison.

Interactive inputs:
- Outside temperature.
- Warehouse thermal load.
- Cooling power.
- Operating duration.
- Electricity prices for different periods.
- Pre-cooling schedule.

Simulation:
- Calculate baseline refrigeration energy and electricity cost.
- Simulate an alternative cooling schedule.
- Compare energy consumption, cost, and estimated emissions.
- Display the estimated savings and a clear explanation.

Important: Do not assume that shifting cooling automatically reduces energy consumption. Calculate the cost benefit from the selected tariff and model assumptions. Respect validated food-storage temperature limits, and never recommend unsafe temperature excursions or uncontrolled refrigeration shutdowns.

## 8. SHARED DIGITAL TWIN ENGINE

Create a reusable simulation engine shared across the four modules.

Every module must have:
1. Editable input controls.
2. A baseline scenario.
3. A Run Simulation or Optimize button.
4. An optimized scenario.
5. Before-and-after charts.
6. Energy, cost, and emissions comparisons.
7. Estimated savings.
8. An explainable recommendation panel.
9. A reset-to-default option.

Calculations must update based on user input.

Use transparent formulas:
- Energy cost = electricity consumed × applicable tariff.
- Emissions = electricity consumed × applicable carbon intensity.
- Energy savings = baseline energy − optimized energy.
- Cost savings = baseline cost − optimized cost.
- Emissions reduction = baseline emissions − optimized emissions.
- Renewable utilization = useful renewable energy consumed ÷ available renewable energy × 100, where applicable.

Use appropriate time-dependent calculations where necessary. Keep units consistent and document all assumptions. Do not force savings to be positive if an optimization performs worse.

## 9. GENAI-STYLE RECOMMENDATION PANEL

- Estimated energy, cost, or carbon benefit.

Label recommendations as simulated and estimated. Structure the code so a real GenAI service can be integrated later.
